# City coverage: where to add data next

Generated by `npm run city-audit` (`scripts/auditCityCoverage.ts`). Full method and
caveats are in the script's header comment; this file is the resulting work-list.

## Headline numbers

- **550** map areas were flagged by the audit (missing coverage in at least one
  era band, or excused from needing any).
- **475** are genuine gaps: a city is plausible there by
  `hasCapability('urban_settlement', …)` and none is recorded for at least one
  of the four era bands.
- **40** are correctly empty — open water, or a map area the geography data
  itself rates as having negligible economic activity (desert interior, ice).
  These are excluded from the ranking below on purpose; adding a city to the
  Empty Quarter or the Caribbean Sea would be a new bug, not a fix.
- Of the ~560 total map areas, roughly a third (~177, per the task brief)
  carry any `CITIES_DATA` entry at all. The remaining gap is concentrated in
  a handful of cultural zones, not spread evenly — see below.

One methodological note that changes the ranking a great deal: the "expected"
test uses `urban_settlement`, not `settled_agriculture`. Farming and a city
are not the same date anywhere — MENA had farming villages roughly 4,000
years before it had Uruk or Memphis — so gating on agriculture alone flagged
"no city before 3000 BCE" almost everywhere on the map, which is simply
correct: nowhere but Mesopotamia and the Nile had cities that early. Once the
test uses the actual urbanization date per zone, the "pre-3000 BCE" band
mostly drops out and the real gaps concentrate in the "3000 BCE – 500 CE"
band, i.e. classical-antiquity capitals and port towns that were never added.

## Top gaps, ranked by population weight

Weight is the average of `regionPopulationWeight × areaPopulationWeight`
sampled across thirteen representative years spanning the app's whole date
range — a rough, defensible stand-in for "how often does the generator
actually land here", not a literal population figure. Higher weight means
more personas are silently missing a birthplace city today.

missing in every era band including the present. Given three thousand years

### Oceania — real gaps, but small in weight

New Guinea and Melanesia (Highlands of Papua, Bismarck Archipelago, Solomon
Islands Chain, Coral Sea Coast, Kokoda Plateau) and several of Australia's

above, because population in these areas is and was thin; a small-density
town would be historically honest and enough to close the gap.

## Data errors found along the way

### Cities keyed to a map area that does not exist (20 keys, highest-value fix)

These are worse than a gap: the city is written into `CITIES_DATA` but under
a key that has no matching area name anywhere in `GEOGRAPHICAL_DATA`, so
`areaPopulationWeight` and `localCity` can never see it. Some are keyed to a
*region* name instead of a specific area (`"Central Europe"`, `"Iberian
Peninsula"`, `"Nile Valley"`, `"The Caribbean"`, `"Nubian Corridor"`, `"Ural
and Arctic Europe"`); one is a diacritic mismatch (`"Yucatan Peninsula"` vs.
the real area `"Yucatán Peninsula"`); the rest have no matching area at all
under any spelling. Real cities affected include **Warsaw, Krakow, Prague,
Frankfurt, Havana, Santo Domingo, Seoul, Pyongyang, Busan, Dublin, Genoa,
Memphis, Khartoum, Persepolis, Calcutta, Cancún, and Panama City** — several
of these are otherwise entirely absent from the generator (Warsaw and Havana
have no other entry anywhere in the table).

Prague to a Central Europe area would help close the Bohemian Plateau/Danube
Bend gap, and Frankfurt could close part of the Germanic Lands gap.

### The same city under two different regions (7 cases)

`Gondar`, Ethiopia's highland fortress-capital, is also filed under `Limpopo
Valley` in Southern Africa — two thousand miles away and a different
cultural region — which is a plain copy-paste error, not a duplicate name.
`Benin City` (West African Forests) is also filed under `Lagos Coastal Belt`
(Upper Guinea), which is more plausible geographically but still worth a
second look. The others (`Boston` under both Boston Harbor and Cape Cod,
`Quebec City` under St. Lawrence River and Champlain Valley, `Palermo` under
Bay of Naples and Sicily, `Angkor`/`Ayutthaya` under two adjacent Mainland
Southeast Asia regions) are geographically closer together and more likely
intentional, but were not individually verified.

### Two duplicate area names across regions

`"Chesapeake Bay"` is defined as a map area under **both** the "Southeast"
region and the "Atlantic Coast" region of North America (`geography.ts` lines
220 and 278). `CITIES_DATA` is keyed on the bare area name, so Jamestown —
filed once, under `"Chesapeake Bay"` — is silently attributed to whichever of
the two regions asks for it. `"Outer Banks"` and `"Rio Grande Valley"` have
the same duplicate-area-name problem, though without city data attached the
practical effect is limited to population weight being counted twice.

### Cities standing forever: the Chang'an and Jamestown cases, confirmed

Both examples named for this task check out:

- **Chang'an**, under `Yellow River Valley`, has no `declineYear`. Its last
  `allegianceHistory` entry is 618 CE (Tang dynasty), and there is no Xi'an
  (or any successor) entry anywhere in the table — so a persona born in the
  Yellow River Valley in 1950 is still, per this data, born in Han/Tang-era
  Chang'an.
- **Jamestown**, under `Chesapeake Bay`, has no `declineYear`. Its last
  allegiance entry is 1776, and Jamestown itself was largely abandoned by the
  1700s in reality — there is no successor entry (Williamsburg, Richmond,
  Norfolk) for the area.

This cannot be told apart from correct cases by the schema alone: Beijing,
London, Paris, Rome, Damascus and Cairo also have no `declineYear`, and are
right not to, because they are still those same cities today. The audit
script prints every no-`declineYear` city whose last documented year is
before 1900 as a **candidate list for review**, oldest first (run with
`--verbose` for the full ~250-entry list rather than the top 20). Beyond the
two confirmed cases above, the oldest and most clearly worth a second look
are Olduvai (Serengeti Plain, founded −2,000,000 with no decline — a
Paleolithic site being treated as a going concern), Tyre (Mount Lebanon
Range, last entry 638), Thebes (Thebes Valley, last entry 641), and Antioch
(Cilician Plain, last entry 1268, no successor recorded).
