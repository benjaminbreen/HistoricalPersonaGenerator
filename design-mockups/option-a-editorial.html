Mary Holloway

A careful neighborhood midwife whose work makes her both trusted and vulnerable in a watchful parish.

Mary Holloway, forty-five and widowed, rents two rooms in Stepney. Her practical knowledge of herbs and difficult births gives her a modest living—and a reputation that must be protected as carefully as her small stock of linen and tinctures.

When a hidden confinement reaches the parish court, she speaks with guarded precision: not a moral arbiter, but a witness who understands how quickly a neighbor’s trouble can become her own.
